**Subject:** Archival clarification/data for W. L. Cahoon's 1971 thesis — Greeves MK.1 only

Dear Library / Engineering Archives team,

I am preparing an independently reproducible comparison of a two-stroke engine
simulation with published experimental measurements. I already have the complete
264-page institutional PDF of W. L. Cahoon's November 1971 PhD thesis,
*Unsteady gas flow through a naturally aspirated two stroke internal combustion
engine*, supervised by G. P. Blair, Queen's University Belfast:

https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/unsteady-gas-flow-through-a-naturally-aspirated-two-stroke-intern/

Institutional file identifier: **612264914**. All PDF page numbers below count
the repository cover as page 1. My question is whether associated appendices,
input sheets, corrections, laboratory notebooks or original records survive;
I am not requesting another copy of the thesis.

The request is limited to the **Greeves 380 cc MK.1** and these unresolved items:

1. **Crankcase free volume and definition.** The specification table (printed
   p.11 / PDF 19) and THROUGH FLOW input sheet (PDF 253, Fig. A2.2) give a
   crankcase compression ratio of 1.6. Is its definition explicitly Vmax/Vmin?
   Which gas cavities and inlet/transfer passages are included? A measured
   free volume at a known crank angle, or a documented volume law with the
   inclusion boundaries, would resolve this; confidential full CAD is not needed.
   Figs. 3.16 and A3.1 (PDF 73 and 260) show the model boundaries, but not the
   absolute volume or measurement convention used for that ratio.

2. **Which MK.1 exhaust dimensions belong to the measured runs?** The main
   specification (PDF 19) gives D1=1.94 in, D9=2.92 in, D2=D3=3.75 in and
   L9=22 in. The input example explicitly described as MK.1 at 4400 RPM
   (Appendix 2, printed p.67 / PDF 249; Fig. A2.2, PDF 254–255) gives
   D1=1.95 in, D9=2.94 in, D2=D3=3.625 in and K9=23 in. Is there an erratum,
   revision note or input sheet identifying the pipe used for the published
   MK.1 experimental pressure/performance records? The actual twin-primary
   junction details, rather than only the equivalent single pipe, would help
   if a dimensioned drawing survives.

3. **Remaining MK.1 flow geometry.** The bore, stroke, rod length, timings,
   total effective port widths, carburettor diameter and overall duct lengths
   are already published. Missing are the complete transfer cross-sections/
   volume, port profiles/radii/angles and confirmation of the detailed inlet
   sheet's association with the experimental build. The existing example
   already gives inlet maximum height 27 mm, downdraft 18 degrees and inlet
   distances K6=2 in / K7=5 in (PDF 253/256); I am asking for identity or
   missing detail, not those published values again. Dimensioned sensor
   positions would also permit comparing pressures at the correct locations.

4. **A coherent experimental operating point, preferably the group near
   6000 RPM.** Table 4.1 (PDF 79) gives the 6000 RPM brake performance, AFR
   and BSFC; Fig. 4.13 (PDF 91) gives cylinder pressure at 6000 RPM.
   Figs. 4.20/4.27 (PDF 98/105) are labelled 5960 RPM, while
   Figs. 4.41/4.48 (PDF 119/126) are labelled 5980 RPM. If available,
   please identify associated test records with actual speed, ignition timing,
   ambient pressure/temperature, fuel properties, carburettor setting, measured
   exhaust temperatures and applied SAE correction factors. If these are
   separate acquisitions, their individual settings are sufficient: they will
   not be presented as simultaneous or silently assigned the same RPM.

Original pressure/torque/airflow/fuel records and calibration notes for one
identified point would be useful if they survive. We do not require a new
experiment, a full speed campaign or data from the MK.2/MK.3 engines. A finding
that these records do not survive, or referral to the relevant collection and
its access conditions, would also close the archival question.

The intent is to retain the measured configuration and report any remaining
uncertainty, not to infer missing dimensions or tune a model to the power curve.
Please advise any access or reuse conditions; no reproduction permissions are
presumed.
